## 1. The 60-second pitch

Every organisation in this room has bought Microsoft 365 Copilot licences, and almost none of
them can tell you who is actually using them. Microsoft's own admin centre gives you a headline
number and nothing you can act on. So the usual answer is a Power BI report built on top of the
Power Platform — which means more licences, a second platform to look after, and your Copilot
data sitting somewhere outside the systems you already trust.

This replaces all of that. It is a single self-hosted web dashboard that reads Copilot usage
straight out of Microsoft Graph, stores it in your own database, and shows you who is using
Copilot, in which apps, how often, and — the question everyone actually asks — who is holding a
licence and has never sent a prompt. No Power BI licence. No Power Platform. Nothing leaves
your own Azure subscription, and the text of what people typed is never stored at all.

What it costs to run is a small managed database on the cheapest burstable tier and two small
containers — the whole thing deploys from one button into a resource group you choose, and the
code is free and open source under an MIT licence. It is a community project, so there is no
Microsoft support agreement behind it; you own it the way you own any internal tool.

It is for the person who has to walk into an executive meeting and justify the Copilot spend.

## 4. Three questions executives will ask

**"Where does our data go?"**
Nowhere. It is deployed into your own Azure subscription, reads from your own Microsoft Graph,
and writes to your own database. Nothing is sent to any third party, there is no vendor cloud
in the middle, and the text of people's prompts is never stored at all — only who, when, and in
which app. The credential it uses to read Graph is encrypted at rest and can be replaced but
never read back out of the product.

**"Do we need Power BI, Fabric or the Power Platform?"**
No — and that is the point of it. The version this replaces needed all of that. This is one
container and one database; the dashboard is part of the application. If your organisation has
never bought the Power Platform, nothing here asks you to start.

**"How long does it take to deploy, and who supports it?"**
The deployment itself is one button in the Azure portal and takes about fifteen minutes; you
type one password and everything else is generated for you. The real work is the permissions:
someone with Global Administrator rights has to create an app registration and approve two
read-only permissions, which is a thirty-minute conversation with your identity team, and
there is a script in the product that does the rest. On support — be straight about this — it
is a free, open-source community project under an MIT licence. There is no Microsoft support
agreement and no service level agreement. You own it like any internal tool.

## 5. If it breaks

**◐ Cold start — the normal case.** The page sits blank or spinning for up to a minute, then
loads completely and works perfectly from then on. Say, honestly:

> "These demos are set to sleep when nobody's on them, so it's just waking up — give it thirty
> seconds. In your own tenant it would be running all the time."

Then keep talking. Do not refresh repeatedly; that does not make it faster.

**○ Genuinely down — rare.** You waited a full two minutes, refreshed twice, and you are
getting an error page or a connection failure rather than a slow load. Do not try to debug it
at the booth. Switch to the fallback and say:

## 6. What NOT to promise

- **It needs admin-consented Microsoft Graph application permissions.** Two of them —
  reading Copilot interaction history, and reading the directory. Both are read-only, both are
  application-level rather than per-user, and both need a Global Administrator to approve them.
  If someone says "our security team will never allow that", do not argue — say "then this
  isn't for you yet", because without those two permissions the product collects nothing.
- **It needs Copilot licences already assigned.** It reports on what is there. It cannot tell
  you anything about a tenant that has not started.
- **It is a desktop dashboard.** There is no phone layout and none is planned. Do not hand your
  phone to anyone to show it.
- **Prompt text is not available — by design.** If someone asks "can we see what people are
  asking it", the answer is no, and that is deliberate. (The Prompt Analyser, the second
  product on the landing page, scores prompt *quality* without the same exposure — point them
  at that stand-up instead of promising a feature.)
- **There is no support contract.** MIT licence, community project, no service level agreement
  and no Microsoft backing. Never imply otherwise to a regulated customer.
- **Do not quote a dollar figure for running it.** Describe the shape — smallest burstable
  database tier, two small containers — and let their own team price it.

## Shutting the demo down after the event

The four demos live in the **MVP 1k** Azure subscription, in four resource groups:
`rg-copilot-usage-demo`, `rg-copilot-prompts-demo`, `rg-copilot-agents-demo`,
`rg-copilot-cowork-demo`, plus `rg-copilot-demos-site` for the landing page.

The container apps are already set to `minReplicas: 0`, so they cost nothing while
nobody is looking at them. **The Postgres flexible servers are not** — they bill
whether or not anyone visits.

◐ **Stopping a Postgres flexible server is not permanent.** Azure automatically
starts a stopped server again after **7 days**. If you stop them and forget, they
quietly resume billing a week later.

● **The durable option is to delete the resource groups** once the event is over.
Everything is reproducible — each repo ships its own one-click template, so standing
the demo back up later is a template deployment and a `seed-demo` call, not a rebuild.

Ask Loryan before deleting anything: the same subscription hosts unrelated work.
